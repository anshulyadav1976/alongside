# Psychological and medical-science basis

## Evidence posture

Alongside is informed by psychological research. It is not a clinical intervention and must not claim that combining these mechanisms creates a validated treatment.

Research comes from different populations and contexts. Product language must use “informed by” rather than “clinically proven”.

## Design principles supported by research

### Behavioural activation

Structured engagement in manageable, rewarding, necessary, or value-linked activity can help people experiencing depressive symptoms. Product implication: offer one small, specific action scaled to capacity, not generic productivity advice.

### Psychological flexibility and acceptance

Acceptance-based approaches focus on allowing difficult internal experiences while continuing value-consistent action. Product implication: do not treat every painful feeling as a problem to eliminate.

### Meaning, hope, and values

Meaning-centred and resilience interventions in serious illness support careful exploration of purpose, identity, agency, and pathways. Product implication: meaning is invited with permission, never imposed or manufactured.

### Positive emotion and savouring

Positive-affect interventions suggest that small positive experiences can coexist with serious stress. Product implication: capture and revisit small good moments without using “at least” language or invalidating pain.

### Grief oscillation

The Dual Process Model describes movement between loss-oriented and restoration-oriented coping. Product implication: temporary distraction and ordinary life can be legitimate respite rather than automatically classified as avoidance.

### Narrative, dignity, and life review

Dignity and life-review interventions show potential value for identity, legacy, generativity, and family meaning, while effects on distress are not universal. Product implication: make narrative/legacy tools optional and avoid efficacy claims.

### Empathy and autonomy

Empathy is associated with better therapeutic outcomes, while autonomy-supportive communication respects choice. Product implication: reflect before solving, ask one question at a time, offer choices, and allow rejection.

### Just-in-time adaptation

JITAI evidence supports context-sensitive intervention timing but average mental-health effects are modest and burden matters. Product implication: receptivity, cooldown, and `NO_ACTION` are first-class policy variables.

## Support-mode rules

| Mode | Appropriate when | Agent behaviour | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Witness | User wants to speak or feels overwhelmed | Reflect, validate, clarify desired help | Immediate fixing, interrogation |
| Regulate | Acute non-crisis overwhelm | Slow pace, orient to next minutes, offer brief grounding | Long unsolicited exercises |
| Restore | User wants respite | Familiar low-effort distraction or pleasant activity | Framing respite as denial |
| Gentle activation | Low activity/energy and user accepts help | One tiny chosen action | “Exercise fixes everything” |
| Savour | User is receptive to noticing a positive moment | Capture sensory detail or repeatable good moment | Forced gratitude |
| Meaning | User invites deeper reflection | Explore values and identity carefully | Inventing purpose for suffering |
| Narrative | User wants continuity or legacy | Journal, story, letter, timeline | Forced trauma retelling |
| Human connection | User wants outside support | Choose person, draft request, rehearse | Replacing people with AI |
| Practical | Logistics are creating burden | Questions, plan, paperwork, next step | Turning every issue emotional |

## Markers the system may track

These are tailoring variables, not diagnostic labels.

### Momentary

- User-requested support mode
- Distress, user-reported or explicitly uncertain inference
- Energy/fatigue
- Arousal or numbness
- Time available
- Social capacity
- Sense of agency/control
- Receptivity to suggestions
- Sleep quality, optional
- Immediate burden

### Contextual

- Difficult event and date
- Upcoming appointment/deadline/anniversary
- Recent changes
- People involved
- Practical obstacles
- User-stated expectations about difficult moments

### Durable, only when explicit

- Values
- Important roles
- Preferred support style
- Repeatedly helpful strategies
- Repeatedly unhelpful strategies
- Trusted people and support types
- Boundaries
- Check-in permissions

### Intervention outcome

- Support mode
- Exact suggestion
- Rationale
- Context
- Accepted/attempted/completed
- Immediate helpfulness
- Burden
- Later effect
- Reuse permission

## Things the system must not infer as facts

- Diagnosis
- Personality disorder
- Attachment style
- Suicidality from vocal tone alone
- Relationship quality without explicit evidence
- “The user is depressed” from several low-mood statements
- Meaning or purpose of suffering

Store observations instead:

> User reported low mood on four evenings this week.

Not:

> User is clinically depressed.

## Conversation style constraints

- Reflect before advising.
- Ask permission before advice when intent is unclear.
- Ask no more than one substantive question per turn.
- Keep voice responses short enough to interrupt.
- Avoid repeated question endings.
- Use memory sparingly.
- Ask permission before sensitive memory references.
- End with a real-world step or respectful closure, not emotional dependency language.

## Claims policy

Allowed:

- “The interaction design is informed by behavioural activation, acceptance-based approaches, meaning-centred work, grief research, and just-in-time intervention design.”

Not allowed:

- “This treats depression.”
- “This is clinically proven.”
- “This prevents suicide.”
- “This is a therapist.”
